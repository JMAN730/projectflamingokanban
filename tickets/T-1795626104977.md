## Original body

Follow-up to #841 on NVIDIA. I substituted a 2560x1440 scene target into the
presented plane and reached the world's pixels, but the device is removed
between 0 and 94 s into the world, so I could not capture a frame. Two
questions at the end: whether the substitution itself shortens the time to
removal, and which of the eight 1440p targets is the finished scene.


MACHINE AND TREE

Windows 11 26200, Ryzen 7 8845H, RTX 4060 Laptop 8 GB, driver 32.0.16.2124,
laptop with the AMD 780M present but unused. Branch black-scene: 59ed4db
(buffer views kept inside their host copy, view heap capped before creation)
plus the substitution below (abde498), --profile diag --features diagnostics,
Series X mode, title 1.0.2.1 (b99f876b...). Full screen at 1920x1080, ray
tracing off, medium preset, motionBlur and waterReflectionSSR off.


WHAT THE SUBSTITUTION DOES

In the queue's PresentX handler, the first plane's resource is replaced with a
texture the same frame wrote, while the remaining planes (HUD, subtitles) are
passed through unchanged.

Candidates are the frame's 2560x1440 f28/f29/f87/f91 targets, noted from
OMSetRenderTargets and UAV binds. A menu or a loading card writes none of them,
so menus stay exactly as the title drew them; an earlier version gated on "the
frame wrote 12 or more targets" instead, a loading card passed that gate, its
plane was replaced, and the device was removed at 141 s, before the world.

The best mode reads one candidate back per frame (copy to a readback buffer,
sample every 64th pixel, count samples above 8) and takes the first with more
than 2 per cent non-zero. The cycle mode rotates through every target of at
least 720p. A number picks one DXGI format. It is opt-in:
VICTORY_PRESENT_TARGET=best, cycle or a format number.

This is the same idea as mpDom's VICTORY_PRESENT_1440P (d6897fa), which reports
visible world geometry on RDNA 2. I did not use that branch directly because my
tree is a different base; the substitution point (sources[0]) and the candidate
formats are the same.


WHAT HAPPENS ON THE 4060

Three runs, each reaching the world and then losing the device. The slow host
submission line is the last one before the removal in every case, and the
submission it reports took 17 to 19 s, so the stall begins that much earlier.

Run 1: world reached 317.8 s after launch, removal 412 s, about 1,400 draws a
frame at entry, slow submission 17,119 ms over 13 lists, 94 s of visible world.

Run 2: world reached 142.2 s, removal 160 s, 2,818 draws a frame, slow
submission 18,629 ms over 19 lists, 18 s of visible world.

Run 3: world reached 123.4 s, removal 123 s, 1,638 draws a frame, slow
submission 18,657 ms over 27 lists, 0 s of visible world.

All three end with 0x887A0004 and 0x887A0006 and no breadcrumbs, at frame 2,048
and up. The world-entry time is where my driver sees 800 or more draws a frame,
so the gap between the two columns is the whole life of the visible world: 94,
18 and 0 s here. Without the substitution the same tree holds the world for
more than three minutes before the same removal, so on this card the fallback
appears to bring the hang forward rather than cause a different one. Two
candidate reasons: the extra 14 MB read back per frame while probing, and the
plane-0 texture changing size against a 3840x2160 back buffer, a compose rather
than a copy.

Captures during the world are mean 0.0 with std 0.32 to 0.34, flat, and
consistent with the frame arriving after the device was already gone.


WHAT THE READ BACK FOUND

The probe did find a target with pixels, which is the useful part:

  the scene target holds pixels resource=...c2940 format=28 filled=0.025

2.5 per cent of samples non-zero is far below the density #841 reports for the
finished scene (dense per-pixel noise, mean about 148 over a 2560x1440 target).
So this is probably one of the sparse G-buffer-ish 1440p targets rather than the
composited one, and my "first above 2 per cent" rule stopped too early. The
probe also needs one frame per candidate, and at 0.5 to 13 fps that is 1 to 2 s
per candidate, which is most of the window before removal.


QUESTIONS

1. Does the substitution shorten the time to removal on NVIDIA? I see 0 to 94 s
with it and minutes without. If others see the same, the read back should move
off the present path: a small centre crop instead of a full 14 MB copy, or a
target nominated by the title's own draw order rather than measured.

2. Which of the eight 2560x1440 colour targets is the finished scene? In #841 the
dump shows two of them with content (f28 and f87, mean 148 and 147). My probe
found a third one at 2.5 per cent. If someone with a working dump can say which
of them the composite reads, the selection becomes a lookup instead of a
measurement.


WHAT DID NOT WORK

Gating on the number of targets the frame wrote, 12 or more: a loading card
passed the gate, its plane was replaced, and the device was removed at 141 s.
Cycle, rotating through every target of at least 720p every 64 frames: the swap
chain resizes at each change and the device was removed at 173 s, before the
world.

No game assets, screenshots or shader data are attached; the numbers and the log
line above are all from my own runs. The full run logs (about 9 MB each) are
available on request.
