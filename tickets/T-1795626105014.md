## Original body

VIctory runs on a Mac under CrossOver, with Apple's D3DMetal as the D3D12. It gets through the menus and the intro video at about 30 fps and into the world, where the first mission's title card and opening credits draw over a scene that is still mostly dark. Posting what it took and what stops it, since I haven't seen a macOS report here and most of it is Apple specific.

**Host:** MacBook Pro, Apple M5 Max, 64 GB, macOS 27.3, CrossOver 26.3 (Wine + Rosetta 2 + D3DMetal). Cross-built with mingw (`x86_64-pc-windows-gnu`), which needed no source changes. No Windows machine or AMD / NVIDIA GPU involved.

**Code:** an older version of the Mac patch is public, 14 commits on `015d45b`: [Ahmet-Dedeler:mac-crossover](https://github.com/Ahmet-Dedeler/VIctory/tree/mac-crossover). The numbers below are from a newer one, 13 commits on top of `BeachesOfViceCity/VIctory-Linux` at `f1e94ce` (`main` `ffe310e` plus their Wine/Proton commits). That one isn't published yet. I know PRs are limited to collaborators, so tell me if a branch on current `main` would be more useful and which parts you'd want.

### How far it gets

- First frame 2 to 3 s after start, sign-in bar in 21 to 30 s.
- All first-run pages and the intro video at about 30 fps (33.0 to 33.3 ms per frame), in about 3 GB.
- With the terms already accepted: loading screens with artwork, the world starts 125 to 150 s after start at about 8.5 to 8.9 GB, and the "VICE CITY, TWO YEARS AGO" title card draws.
- In the world: a horizon line, then street and car lights with bloom, the rest black. About 6 s per frame. I played it by hand for about 15 minutes, up to the opening credits drawing over the scene. Then the title ended itself: its stall watchdog had fired 8 times (the patch skips it), and after that title threads raised a breakpoint exception at `+0x63966f`. I don't know yet what that site checks.

### What stops it

1. **The scene stays dark even with indirect draws running.** The argument clamp (#170) builds here with `dxcompiler.dll` and `dxil.dll` next to the exe (#178's route). After that 0 indirect executions are dropped for a missing clamp and 2,100 to 2,900 draws reach the host per frame, but the picture is the same. Still dropped per world frame in one 140 s run: 445 to 911 where the compute shader doesn't translate (same as Windows), 255 indirect draws with "prepare failed" although the pipeline exists, 124 to 151 compute pipelines that need 64-lane waves, 60 to 81 where the mesh shader doesn't translate, 43 for a pixel shader. I haven't worked out which of these keeps the lit scene from showing.
2. **Metal's mesh output limit.** A mesh pipeline whose outputs go over 32,768 bytes (as Metal counts them) is created by D3DMetal without an error and never finishes when used. 340 of the title's mesh shaders are over it. The patch doesn't create those pipelines and drops their draws.
3. **64-lane compute.** D3DMetal only runs 32-lane waves and turns a `WaveSize(64)` compute pipeline into a no-op, silently. That is 321 of the 1,685 translated compute shaders. I have a 32-lane translation written (off by default), checked with Apple's IR verifier only. None of it has run on the GPU.
4. **No write tracking of title memory on this host**, so everything a draw can read is copied again every frame, 7 to 8.7 GB per world frame. That is a big part of the 6 s.
5. **GPU hang on plain `main`.** On `ffe310e` plus the same patch the world ends about 40 s in: macOS logs `kIOGPUCommandBufferCallbackErrorHang` and the runtime reports "the host GPU did not finish a submission of 26 lists in 12 s". With VIctory-Linux's bounded one-view tables for root SRVs / UAVs it didn't happen in the runs I made. I haven't isolated the cause.

### Things about Apple's stack that may save someone time

- **Apple's DXIL converter merges the per-channel stores of one mesh output element into a single vector write, placed at the first store.** If a channel's value is computed between two stores (an f16 conversion in our case), the vector uses a value defined after it. The Apple GPU compiler service then crashes in register allocation, the pipeline becomes a no-op and later compiles fail. 4,693 of 8,191 translated mesh shaders hit this. Fix: compute all of an export's channels before emitting any of its stores. After that, 0.
- **A no-op pipeline is silent, and fatal later.** D3DMetal reports success for a pipeline Metal refused, and a submission that uses it never signals its fence. So the runtime has to decide whether a pipeline can exist on this host and drop its draws.
- **D3DMetal signals fences early**, before the GPU has finished, so a fence wait can't tell you which submission hung.
- The console's 10-bit float formats have no equivalent here. Half floats work as a stand-in.
- Rosetta 2 needs the usual instruction handling (CLWB sites retired after the first trap, and so on).

### Not tested

- VIctory's own test suite on this host.
- Gameplay, and audio (muted in these runs).
- No frame was compared against a console capture.
- Frame times are from a machine that was also running other builds for part of the session, so the idle figure could be better than 6 s.

The title's network is blocked on every run, and the terms page was accepted by hand.

What I found broken on the way to it:

- **Material masks.** `CS_GenerateMaterialMasks` and `CS_MaterialTilesInitial` write a material number per pixel into the depth plane (R16 view) and the stencil plane (R8_UINT view) of a 16-bit depth target with stencil, through unordered access views.
