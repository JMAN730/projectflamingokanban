### Comment 1 — @pickles976 — 2026-11-24T17:01:03Z

Cross-runtime datum that should reshape how these tables get **read**, not how they're built: on the sibling runtime (ivall/xcl), the deepest run in the world — ~5 minutes of in-world play before the title's watchdog — ended with its own context line reading **"16,374 draws with translated pipelines (0 skipped)"**. The kill arrived with the draw path fully translated: shader refusals were *not* what stalled that run (completion-stall class; the shared story). Two consequences for your benchmark's framing:

1. **These counts are not a watchdog countdown.** A refused program mostly yields missing/degraded visuals, not a stall; the stall-relevant subset is "refused **and** the title blocks on that specific work," which offline corpus counts cannot see — the 3,500-row list is the *unplayability* backlog (black screens, invisible geometry), and a much smaller, scene-specific intersection is the *stall* backlog. Review effort should rank by validator-gained AND in-world-reachability, not gained alone.
2. **The pairing that would actually produce the stall-critical number exists on both sides:** your runtime's breadcrumbs already log the **last pipelines bound before a hang** (your #1062/#1222 style output), and both runtimes log their refusal reasons per program. Intersect "hung-run last-bound set" × "refused set" across a handful of runs and you get, for the first time anywhere, the *stall-critical shader count* — I'd bet four figures offline converges to tens of distinct programs per scene, and that would let people stop saying "fix the shaders" as if the answer were 3,500. Happy to run the same intersection on our refusal vocabulary whenever you have three breadcrumb-carrying hang reports to feed it.

Also worth pinning: your "no PR lost a program" line is the most under-appreciated safety property in the weekend's data — it means the merge risk on all 25 is one-directional, which is exactly what a jammed review queue needs to hear.
