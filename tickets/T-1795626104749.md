## Original body

The first cutscene runs at about 1 fps here before the host GPU hang (#71, #325). The diagnostics build's own lines show where the ~0.8 s of a world frame go, and four open PRs that target copies were measured against `main` on the same machine. Numbers only, from VIctory's log lines (`fps, ms/frame (… preparing …)` and `copies per frame: …`).

**Setup:** NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti 16 GB, driver 621.24 (32.0.16.2124), 32-lane waves; AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D, 31 GB; Windows 11 10.0.26100; Rust 1.98.1. Diag build (`--profile diag --features diagnostics`) of each commit, each run with its own empty `VICTORY_CACHE_DIR`, the same `VICTORY_KEYS` (Enter every 3 s from 8 s to 47 s; story mode opens at 24 s in every run), until the host GPU failure. "World" is every frame-rate line under 10 fps, averaged per run.

**`main` `d2a4bae`, three runs** (ms per world frame):

| run | frame | preparing (copies) | textures | on the GPU | copying back | draws/frame | dropped without a pipeline / indirect | device removed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 845 | 399 (304 ms, 186 MB) | 21 | 216 | 110 | 1925 | 1437 / 1949 | 111 s |
| 2 | 815 | 367 (288 ms, 221 MB) | 19 | 322 | 74 | 1830 | 1394 / 1697 | 100 s |
| 3 | 775 | 332 (245 ms, 142 MB) | 29 | 281 | 54 | 1625 | 1750 / 1704 | 59 s |

Per world frame the copy statistics show 33–35 k refreshes asking for 25–29 GB. Write-watch checks 20–30 GB of that (143–165 ms in watches), and 97 % of it is unchanged: about 0.7 GB per frame has actually changed. So most of a frame is the copies into and out of title memory plus the GPU's own time, and about 1,400–1,750 draws per frame wait for a pipeline that is still being built.

**Four open PRs, each merged alone onto `main` `d2a4bae`:**

| PR | frame | preparing (copies) | on the GPU | copying back | device removed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| #124 `a6deb46` | 742 | 360 (282 ms) | 212 | 93 | 115 s |
| #261 `a2243ad` | 765 | 352 (281 ms) | 261 | 66 | 154 s |
| #230 `6a96adb` | 715 | 355 (282 ms) | 259 | 64 | 141 s |
| #322 `f7bfa64`, run 1 / run 2 | 867 / 742 | 305 (219 ms) / 244 (173 ms) | 179 / 287 | 46 / 26 | 65 s / 89 s |

- Only #322 moves a component outside `main`'s three-run range, in both of its runs: copies 173–219 ms against 245–304, preparing 244–305 ms against 332–399, copying back 26–46 ms against 54–110.
- #124, #261 and #230 stay within `main`'s range on every column here (one run each), which may just mean their effect is smaller than the run-to-run spread at 1 fps.
- None of them changes the hang. The time of the device removal varies from 59 to 154 s across all runs, so the PRs' times say nothing either way.
- The whole frame does not get clearly shorter with any of them, because GPU time (179–322 ms) and the draws waiting for pipelines stay.

**Not tested:** AMD GPUs (64-lane waves); release builds; more than one or two runs per PR; the PRs combined.

**AI use:** Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) built each commit, ran the title, summarised the log lines and wrote this report; the numbers come from the runs above.

### Comment 10 — @Mumogg — 2026-11-24T19:08:25Z

Under Wine the world frame is not bound by the title process at all: it is bound by wineserver, and wineserver spends all of its time unmapping views. This is the cause I could not name in my earlier comment here (the calls costing microseconds alone and hundreds of times more inside the title).

**Setup:** RTX 5070 Ti (driver 615.78.08), Ryzen 5 9600X, Nobara 44 (kernel 7.3.4, `/dev/ntsync` present), Wine 11.22 Staging, vkd3d-proton from GE-Proton11-14. Tree: `ffe310e` + #454 #177 #259 #233 #646 #578 #334 #493 #724 #725 + the #783 fallback, diagnostics build, DRED off, wave64 mesh refused (#578), about 220 draws per world frame, about 1 fps.

**1. Who uses the CPU** (`/proc` user+kernel times over a 60 s window in the world):

| | CPU in 60 s |
|---|---|
| wineserver (one thread) | 51.9 s user, 0.4 s kernel |
| the whole title process, all threads | 30.3 s user, 2.7 s kernel |
| its busiest thread | 6.8 s + 0.6 s |

So the title's threads are mostly waiting, and the single-threaded server is at about 85% of a core.

**2. What wineserver does** (`perf record` on it, user space, 40 s in the world, 17,012 samples):

| symbol | share |
|---|---|
| `is_valid_view_addr` | 57.2% |
| `req_unmap_view` | 42.7% |
| everything else | 0.1% |

**3. Why** (counters added in `xmem.rs`, per 5 s in the world):

| | |
|---|---|
| views held in `Physical::views` | 92,000 to 100,600 |
| views unmapped (`UnmapViewOfFile2`) | 500 to 5,700 per 5 s |
| `map_alias` calls / pages | 630 to 3,400 / 280 to 3,300 (nearly always one page per call) |

The title remaps its pool a 64 KiB page at a time, so the process ends up with about 100,000 views, and each unmap appears to make the server walk its list of the process's views (50 s of CPU over roughly 20,000 to 60,000 unmaps in the window is 1 to 2.5 ms per unmap, 10 to 25 ns per view). On Windows the kernel finds a view by address in a tree; here every other request from every thread also waits behind that walk, which is why reads, write-watch queries and waits all looked slow from inside the title.

**What I tried:** mapping pages that follow one another in a section as one view in `map_alias` (a local switch). No effect, as the counters predict: the runs are one page long (1,010 pages in 1,010 runs), the view count stays at 92,000 to 100,000, wineserver stays at 50.2 s per 60 s, the frame at about 1,000 ms.

**What would help, as far as I can see:** fewer live views (merging neighbours that show consecutive pages back into one view, which costs unmaps of its own), or fewer unmaps, or a view lookup in wineserver that is not linear. The last one is Wine's to fix. Edit: I have now read `server/mapping.c` (upstream 11.21; Staging may differ): a process's views are a linked list, `find_mapped_view` (unmap) searches it linearly, and `is_valid_view_addr`, called for every new view, walks the whole list to check for overlap. So both a map and an unmap cost a pass over all ~100,000 views. AMD and Proton users should see the same thing, since it does not depend on the GPU; a `top` during the world showing wineserver near 100% of a core would confirm it.
