### System Specifications
- **GPU:** AMD Radeon RX 9070 XT (16 GB VRAM)
- **CPU:** Intel Core i5-14600K
- **RAM:** 32 GB (2x16 GB) DDR4 @ 3300 MHz
- **OS:** Windows 11 (64-bit)
- **Branch:** `main` (latest release build)

### Steps to Reproduce / Observed Behavior
1. Launch via `victory.exe run <exe_path> --execute`.
2. Intro video begins playing cleanly with synchronized audio at 30 FPS.
3. **Important:** From the start, I held **A** on the gamepad 3 to 4 times to consecutively skip intros/disclaimers. Hold-to-skip input registers smoothly.
4. Game transitions to world loading, and the first mission's title card successfully renders on screen.
5. Immediately after the title card, the 3D scene remains dark/black, rendering extremely slowly at ~0.5 to 1.8 seconds per frame (SPF).
6. After roughly 50–55 seconds of trying to present frames, the Windows display driver encounters a TDR fault and resets (screen flickers black, process terminates).

### Comment 7 — @GamingRipperDoc — 2026-11-24T04:26:51Z

@KingKaom "Location-independent" matches what #336 found. On my RX 9070 XT, all three of my world-entry faults on `d2a4bae` (+ #175) logged `no free dynamic descriptor slots: a table is not made` about 1 s before the device was removed, and with #336 that warning disappears and the world lasts about 70–175 s instead of 8–38 s (details on #336). When dynamic slots run out, whatever draw comes next reads stale descriptors, so the faulting pipeline changes every time and skipping some just moves it, as you saw.

Worth checking whether that warning shows in your log before the removal (`grep "no free dynamic descriptor slots"`), and trying #336. With it, the later removal I get is a different one (page fault in a freed texture, ~1–3 min in).
